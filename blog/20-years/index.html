Staged4Sale Celebrates 20 Years: How a North County Stager Weathered the Housing Crash, a Pandemic and the Rise of AI

Oceanside staging pioneer combines traditional staging with new technology instead of fighting it

By Staged4Sale • November 1, 2026 • Press Release

OCEANSIDE, Calif. — Nov. 1, 2026 — When Tawni Oppenheim opened Staged4Sale in 2006, the housing market was near its peak. Then came the crash, the recovery and a pandemic. Now, as AI changes what buyers expect, Staged4Sale marks 20 years by pairing new technology with the real rooms and trained eye at the heart of staging.

A Pioneer's Start

Oppenheim is one of San Diego's home staging pioneers. Staged4Sale was born one evening in 2006, when she realized she could combine her eye for design with her desire to help create wealth for people. That year, when professional staging was still new to many sellers, she trained at the Staging University in Chicago under Barb Schwarz, widely credited as the founder of home staging. Today she is an ASPM Master Stager (Accredited Staging Professional Master).

"I was a pioneer minus the stagecoach, horses and fancy hat, with no social media, no AI, and no blueprint to follow... I printed out driving directions from MapQuest and hoped for the best," Oppenheim wrote on the company's Facebook page.

Twenty Years of Ups and Downs

In the 2008 crash and foreclosure era, staging helped sellers stand out as they competed for fewer buyers. The Associated Press featured Oppenheim and her crew transforming a Southern California home in a single day.

"The Great Recession brought its own set of challenges where people were losing homes and short sales were long sales, but it also revealed the true impact of my work. I witnessed firsthand how staging could transform not just homes, but lives," she wrote.

The company carried on through the recovery, COVID-19 and higher mortgage rates.

"The pandemic turned the market upside down overnight. We adapted, kept our crews safe and kept homes selling," Oppenheim said.

Now, in its 20th year, Staged4Sale has a new logo and a larger warehouse. "We are growing again," the company announced on Facebook. Its crew stages vacant and occupied homes from its own furniture inventory, from condos to coastal and luxury homes in Carlsbad, La Jolla and Rancho Santa Fe to a builder's new-construction model. It also offers consultations and Staged4Home redesign for homeowners who aren't selling. Many local agents return listing after listing.

Oppenheim has earned Best of Home Staging honors for Staging Excellence from the International Association of Home Staging Professionals and has appeared on San Diego real estate television. "Staging is far less than any price reduction on your home," she said in one segment.

Looking Ahead

The latest hurdle is AI virtual staging, and Oppenheim, who also offers AI consulting to her clients, treats it as a tool rather than a threat. She continues to search for new ways to combine traditional staging with technology to help both sellers and buyers. Staged4Sale recently revamped its website and is developing more online tools to make working with the company easier for clients and agents.

"Technology should make staging better, not replace it. Every tool we build is about taking friction out of the process for sellers and their agents," Oppenheim said.

Buyers still respond to real rooms. In the National Association of Realtors' 2025 Profile of Home Staging, 83% of buyers' agents said staging made it easier for buyers to envision a property as their future home.

"Virtually staged houses don't allow buyers to make an emotional connection to their potential home," Oppenheim has written. As she puts it: "AI is a great tool, and we use it. But a buyer can't sit on a virtual sofa."
